Product 01 — Financial Inclusion

Daftari — the voice ledger built to scale beyond one language.

MVP Live · Dar es Salaam Pilot

Daftari turns a smartphone into a business notebook a merchant can talk to. A shopkeeper holds the mic, speaks a sale in their own language between customers, and the app handles the rest — categorising the transaction, tracking profit, and building the financial history that formal lenders currently can't see.

We piloted this product under the name "SwahiliSME." We're carrying the name forward as Daftari — the Swahili word for ledger, and one already understood as "record" or "register" across Arabic, Hausa, Somali, and Amharic-speaking trade communities. A name tied to one language limits a product tied to one market. A name tied to a universal act — keeping the books — travels with it.
Rekodi — Voice Ledger

Speak it, don't type it.

Hold-to-record transaction logging in the merchant's own language. A sale that takes two minutes to type takes three seconds to say between customer waves.

Mshauri — AI Advisor

Compliance, explained simply.

A chatbot that answers tax and registration questions in plain language, grounded in the merchant's real numbers. The rule-set behind it is swappable per market, not hardcoded.

Kadi ya Afya — Health Card

A financial history, on demand.

A bank-ready PDF summary generated from real transaction history — the document a lender needs, produced from data a merchant already has.

Offline-First Sync

Works with zero signal.

Transactions queue locally and sync automatically the moment a connection returns. No lost records, no manual "sync" button to remember.

Product 02 — Assistive Communication

NENA — a pocket interpreter for East Africa's Deaf community.

MVP · East Africa · Targeting Q4 2026

NENA is a bidirectional mobile platform that translates between sign language and spoken language in real time — face to face, and over an ordinary phone call — without a human interpreter in the loop. We're building it East-Africa-first and language-agnostic by design, so the same core becomes a global sign-to-speech translator, not a single-market app.

NENA's gesture recognition uses few-shot learning — it doesn't need a massive labelled dataset to understand a new sign language, just a calibration set of a few hundred signs. That's the technical unlock for going global: American Sign Language, British Sign Language, or Nigerian Sign Language become a new calibration pass, not a research project from scratch.
Sign-to-Speech

Signs become spoken words.

The front camera captures a gesture, an on-device model classifies it, and the phone speaks the translation aloud in under two seconds — in the connected spoken language.

Speech-to-Sign

Speech becomes an avatar.

Incoming speech is transcribed and rendered as a 3D avatar signing in near real time, so a Deaf user can follow a hearing speaker without lip-reading.

Nena Call — VoIP Relay

A standard phone call, translated live.

A Deaf user can place or receive an ordinary call, anywhere a phone network reaches. The hearing caller needs no special app — they just hear natural spoken language.

Voice Identity

A voice that matches who they are.

Users choose a Male or Female TTS profile at onboarding and can change it any time — because vocal dignity isn't optional, in any market.

NENA — Path to Global

Sign language is not one language. Neither is this product.

Sign Language Layer

Each sign language — TSL, ASL, BSL, or any other — is a separate few-shot calibration set behind the same recognition engine. New sign languages are added, not built from zero.

Spoken Language Layer

ASR and TTS run as swappable language packs — Whisper already supports dozens of spoken languages, so the "output side" of a translation pair is rarely the constraint.

Call Infrastructure Layer

WebRTC and SIP relay work over any telecom network, so Nena Call scales to a new country as a carrier and SIP-gateway integration — not a rebuilt calling stack.

NENA Expansion Roadmap
Phase 1 — Now

East Africa: TSL & KSL ↔ Swahili.

Prove real-time bidirectional translation and live-call relay in Tanzania and Kenya, with a community-validated 100-sign MVP dictionary and Deaf-led calibration.

Phase 2 — Year 1

Africa-wide: more sign languages, more spoken pairs.

Add Nigerian Sign Language, South African Sign Language, and Ugandan Sign Language, paired against English, French, and additional local spoken languages.

Phase 3 — Year 2

International: the major sign languages.

Calibrate American Sign Language, British Sign Language, and International Sign, opening Nena Call to global carriers and cross-border Deaf communication.

Phase 4 — Year 3+

Universal relay: any sign, any spoken language.

Position NENA as accessibility infrastructure — a relay layer that telecoms, banks, and public services can integrate directly, rather than a single consumer app.

This roadmap reflects strategic intent, not a committed timeline. Each new sign language requires a dedicated calibration and community-validation phase before release — accuracy and dignity come before speed of expansion.

Scalability & Expansion Potential

Built to leave Tanzania on purpose.

A product that only works in one language, under one country's tax code, on one payment rail, is a pilot — not a platform. We designed Daftari's architecture so that expanding into a new market is a configuration exercise, not a rebuild.

Language & Voice Layer

The ASR and NLP extraction stage is a pluggable component, not hardcoded logic. Adding Kenyan Swahili, Luganda, Hausa, or Yoruba is a model swap and a fine-tuning pass — not a rebuild of the app.

Regulatory & Compliance Layer

Mshauri's compliance knowledge lives as an externalised rule-set, not application code. TRA/BRELA/NSSF becomes KRA in Kenya or URA in Uganda by loading a new rule-set, not shipping a new version.

Trade & Payments Layer

The ledger and Health Card model are currency- and rail-agnostic by design, so integrations with mobile money and regional trade frameworks are additions to the roadmap, not assumptions baked into the schema.

Expansion Roadmap
Phase 1 — Now

Tanzania: prove the model.

Daftari runs its pilot in Dar es Salaam's markets — Kariakoo, Tandale, Mwenge — in Swahili, against TRA, BRELA, and NSSF rules, targeting 500 active users and a 65% 90-day retention rate.

Phase 2 — Year 1

East African Community: prove the pattern repeats.

Expand into Kenya and Uganda. Add English–Swahili code-switching support, connect the compliance advisor to KRA and URA rule-sets, and integrate mobile money rails — M-Pesa and Airtel Money — as payment and data sources.

Phase 3 — Year 2

Regional trade corridor: reduce duplicate work.

Add Rwanda and Zambia. Layer in cross-border invoicing so an SME trading within the EAC common market can log a transaction once and satisfy more than one tax authority's records.

Phase 4 — Year 3+

Pan-African: turn history into access to capital.

Localise into West and Southern African languages — Hausa, Yoruba, isiZulu — and offer aggregated, anonymised, consent-based transaction history as an alternative credit-scoring signal for banks and mobile-money lenders operating under the AfCFTA framework.

This roadmap reflects strategic intent, not a committed timeline. Each phase depends on funding, in-market regulatory partnerships, and validated results from the phase before it.

Upcoming Products

Four systems in research or early concept.

Each of these extends an architecture principle NENA and Daftari already prove out — edge-first inference, few-shot learning, offline-first design — into a standalone system. None of these are shipped. Status is labeled honestly below; we'll publish real numbers when we have results worth standing behind, not before.

ZanCore-1

Concept — Research

Inference Optimization Engine

A low-latency, low-cost inference runtime for deploying open-weight language models on edge and cost-constrained GPU hardware — the same latency pressure that shapes NENA and Daftari's cloud-side calls, pulled into its own product.

  • Design goal: sub-50ms time-to-first-token on 8B-class open-weight models via quantization and kernel-level optimization
  • Design goal: meaningfully lower VRAM footprint than FP16 baseline deployment
  • Would directly reduce hosting cost and response latency for Mshauri and NENA's cloud-side inference

ZanFlow-Agent

Concept — Research

Deterministic Agent Runtime

A structured, stateful execution framework for AI agents in workflows where reliability matters more than improvisation — replacing ad hoc prompt chains with explicit state, verification steps, and fallback paths.

  • Design goal: graph-based execution instead of free-form prompt chaining
  • Design goal: real-time output validation with deterministic fallbacks, not silent failure
  • Motivated directly by Mshauri's confidence-gating requirement — don't guess at compliance advice

SwahiliLM

Concept — Data Collection

Regional Language & Speech Model

Both NENA and Daftari currently lean on general-purpose Whisper ASR. A model fine-tuned specifically on Swahili and neighbouring East African languages — including code-switching, regional accents, and financial/compliance vocabulary — is the natural next step once we have the data to train it responsibly.

  • Design goal: materially lower word error rate than general-purpose ASR on Tanzanian/Kenyan Swahili
  • Design goal: CPU-viable quantized runtime, so it can run on the same mid-range hardware NENA and Daftari already target
  • Depends on consented, community-sourced voice data — not a shortcut we'll take before it exists

ZanCommerce

Concept — Validating

Social Commerce Agent & Settlement Bridge

Most East African social commerce runs through Instagram for storefront, WhatsApp for negotiation, and mobile money for payment — three disconnected surfaces a merchant reconciles by hand. ZanCommerce would bridge them into one automated flow, living inside WhatsApp rather than a separate dashboard.

  • Design goal: mixed Swahili/English (Sheng) conversational agent handling product inquiries in-chat
  • Design goal: automated mobile money payment verification, replacing manual screenshot checking
  • Currently in merchant interviews, not development — we're validating the workflow before we build it
